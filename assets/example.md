# Elon Musk engineering algorithm

Elon Musk told Tim Dodd in July 2021 at Starbase about his five steps to design, produce, and stop lying to yourself. Five steps to follow **in order**:

  1. Make the requirements less silly—and assign each requirement to a specific person, not a department.
  2. Eliminate the part or step—*“if you don’t give back at least 10% of what you’ve eliminated, it means you didn’t dare to do it.”*
  3. Simplify or optimize. *And only now.*
  4. Speed up the cycle time.
  5. Automate. Save the best for last.

The order is not trivial.

---

Musk himself recounts having done the sequence in reverse for the Model 3 battery packs: he automated a $2 million robotic cell to lay fiberglass mats, sped up the pace, simplified the subassembly—and then ended up asking what the mats were for. The answer, after an investigation: no one knew. The cell was scrapped; two million down the drain.

This isn’t a story against automation. It’s a story against **reversing the sequence**.

Today, many organizations equate “automating” with “adding an AI agent.” The reverse sequence takes on a different form: we plug an agent into a workflow we’ve never audited, we speed up what we should have eliminated, and we optimize what shouldn’t exist in the first place.
